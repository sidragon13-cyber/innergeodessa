## 1. Purpose

This document creates the first EI candidate-item pool and translates the approved blueprint into traceable draft wording. It does not select the final 18 EI pilot items and makes no reliability or validity claim. Six alternatives per subdimension provide room to revise or remove weak, contaminated, inaccessible, or overlapping items later.

> **Candidate items are written for review and comparison, not immediate public deployment.**

## 2. EI construct boundaries

EI describes relative preferences for energy renewal, internal or external processing, breadth or depth of interaction, expression timing, environmental stimulation, and interaction initiation.

It must not primarily measure confidence, shyness, popularity, loneliness, social anxiety, public-speaking skill, leadership, kindness, communication ability, number of friends, or social status. Extraversion and Introversion are equally legitimate preference patterns, and access to either remains available regardless of the leading result.

## 3. Required subdimensions

The pool contains exactly six candidates for each:

1. Energy renewal.
2. Processing mode.
3. Social breadth and depth.
4. Expression timing.
5. Stimulation preference.
6. Interaction initiation.

## 4. Item balance rules

The 36 candidates use the parent specification’s five-point agreement scale. The pool targets 18 E-keyed and 18 I-keyed items, with both poles in each subdimension. Wording uses plain international English, one main proposition, no double negatives, no forced-choice pairs, and no assumptions about employment, university, family, wealth, or lifestyle.

`Ascending` means agreement contributes toward the recorded keyed preference. `Direct` and `counter-keyed` describe wording strategy, not grammatical positivity and not scoring direction.

## 5. Candidate-item metadata conventions

Every row records a stable candidate ID, family and blueprint trace, subdimension, keyed pole, scoring direction, wording type, item form, behavioural context, wording, three risk ratings, main confound, and reviewer note.

Risk abbreviations are `L` (low), `M` (medium), and `H` (high). These ratings are preliminary review prompts, not empirical findings.

## 6. Item-form requirements

The pool mixes behavioural tendencies, preference statements, scenario responses, comparative preferences, and frequency-framed items. Comparative items are limited because they can imply a false opposition. No item uses a direct type label or vague identity claim.

## 13. Candidate wording quality review

Items explicitly marked for revision remain in the pool so their construct intent and risks are visible rather than silently removed.

## 15. Confound review

Each item table records its most likely confound. These labels guide later review; they do not prove that an item is unusable. Repeated batch-level concerns are:

- Social confidence and cultural politeness in breadth, expression, and initiation.
- Language fluency and hierarchy in processing and expression.
- Current stress, physical fatigue, and access to quiet space in energy renewal.
- Attention or sensory differences in stimulation.
- Group, role, and opportunity constraints across social contexts.
