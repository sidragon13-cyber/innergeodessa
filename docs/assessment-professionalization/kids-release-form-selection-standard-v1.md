**Status:** Release-Form Design Standard
**Production Ready:** No
**Scope:** Ages 6–8 and Ages 9–12

## 2. Why Equal Domain Counts

Each domain must contribute the same number of scored items.

This prevents:

- hidden domain weighting;
- score inflation caused by unequal item counts;
- unnecessary scoring normalisation;
- artificial ranking advantages.

V1 should prefer transparent scoring over complex weighting.

---

## 3. Selection Principle

Items are not selected because they are the most interesting or visually attractive.

Each release item must contribute unique measurement value.

Selection priorities:

1. construct clarity;
2. low contamination;
3. domain differentiation;
4. age appropriateness;
5. low social-desirability risk;
6. low exposure dependency;
7. accessibility;
8. bilingual equivalence;
9. context diversity;
10. low redundancy.

---

## 4. Construct Coverage

Within each domain, selected items should cover multiple expressions of the interest.

Example:

Think should not contain five versions of:

> I like puzzles.

Instead it should cover areas such as:

- pattern reasoning;
- logical relationships;
- classification;
- strategy;
- constrained problem solving.

The same principle applies to every domain.

---

## 5. Redundancy Rule

Two candidate items should not both enter the Release Form when they measure nearly the same activity in nearly the same context.

Prefer:

one strong item

over:

two highly similar items.

Reserve items remain available for future substitution.

---

## 6. Risk Preference

When two items provide similar construct coverage, prefer the item with lower:

- social-desirability risk;
- cultural dependency;
- socioeconomic dependency;
- occupational exposure;
- physical-access dependency;
- language complexity;
- ability contamination.

---

## 7. Persistence Items

Persistence is not a core Kids interest dimension.

Items whose primary meaning becomes:

- persistence;
- conscientiousness;
- frustration tolerance;
- confidence;
- perseverance

should not enter the core Release Form merely to increase item count.

Selected V1 items should primarily measure direct attraction to activities.

---

## 8. Context Balance

The full Release Form should include varied contexts:

- individual activity;
- shared activity;
- creative activity;
- investigation;
- practical making;
- reasoning;
- communication;
- environment;
- active exploration.

No domain should depend only on school-like situations.

---

## 9. Response-Bias Control

Avoid a Release Form where many items have obviously socially preferred responses.

Particular caution:

### Connect

Must not become:

> Are you kind?

### Lead

Must not become:

> Are you confident or popular?

### Think

Must not become:

> Are you smart?

### Build

Must not become:

> Are you technically skilled?

### Move

Must not become:

> Are you athletic?

### Create

Must not become:

> Are you artistic?

### Express

Must not become:

> Are you outgoing?

### Discover

Must not become:

> Are you academically scientific?

---

## 10. Reserve Item Status

Items not selected for Release Form remain:

**Reviewed Candidate Reserve Items**

They may later support:

- item substitution;
- pilot revision;
- alternate forms;
- validation research;
- age refinement;
- future adaptive assessment.

Not selected does not mean failed.

Release Form stores the ordered list of selected candidate IDs.

---

## 12. Scoring Preparation

After item selection:

Ages 6–8:

4 items × 8 domains

Ages 9–12:

5 items × 8 domains

This creates a transparent base for:

- raw domain scores;
- normalised percentages;
- domain ranking;
- score gaps;
- broad-interest detection;
- blended-interest detection;
- emerging-interest profiles.

Scoring thresholds are not defined in this document.

---

## 13. Selection Sequence

Candidate Freeze
→
Domain-by-Domain Selection
→
Redundancy Review
→
Cross-Domain Contamination Review
→
Release Form Freeze
→
Scoring Design

---

## 14. Release Principle

The goal is not:

> use as many questions as possible.

The goal is:

> use enough high-quality questions to produce a stable, understandable interest map without exhausting the child.
