The visual concept set is structurally sound.

The primary remaining risk is not general illustration quality.

It is the boundary between:

> illustrating an activity

and

> asking the child to perform the activity.

The latter can contaminate an interest assessment with ability.

## 2. K68-CREATE-01 — REVISE

**Current construct:** Original visual expression.

**Risk:**  
The concept still centres conventional drawing materials and may visually imply that creative interest means hand drawing.

This creates possible:

- motor-accessibility bias;
- prior art-exposure bias;
- narrow representation of creative expression.

**Preserve:**  
Original visual creation.

**Revision direction:**  
Represent choosing and shaping an original visual idea rather than conventional drawing performance alone.

Possible representation may include:

- arranging large shapes;
- selecting forms;
- moving visual elements;
- drawing as only one of several visible creation methods.

The image must not imply that fine hand control or drawing skill defines Create.

---

## 3. K68-DISCOVER-03 — REVISE

**Current construct:** Observation and evidence seeking.

**Risk:**  
“Finding small clues” can unintentionally depend on visual acuity or fine-detail detection.

That changes the task from:

> Do I enjoy investigating?

toward:

> Can I visually detect tiny details?

**Preserve:**  
Observation and evidence seeking.

**Revision direction:**  
Show active examination of several clear clues or features rather than extremely small details.

Clues should be:

- visually obvious enough for comprehension;
- large enough to avoid visual-acuity testing;
- neutral in attractiveness;
- not dependent on hidden-object performance.

---

## 4. K68-THINK-01 — REVISE

**Current construct:** Pattern reasoning interest.

**Risk:**  
A visible unfinished pattern may become an actual reasoning task.

The child may begin solving it before answering whether they enjoy the activity.

This contaminates interest with reasoning performance.

**Preserve:**  
Interest in pattern-based reasoning.

**Revision direction:**  
Show a child engaging with or arranging pattern pieces.

Do not require the viewer to solve the pattern.

The visual should communicate:

> This is the kind of activity.

not:

> Solve this now.

---

## 5. K68-THINK-02 — REVISE

**Current construct:** Sequencing interest.

**Risk:**  
An open “next position” strongly resembles a scored missing-piece assessment.

This risks measuring:

- sequencing ability;
- performance confidence;
- familiarity with puzzle tests.

**Preserve:**  
Interest in sequencing.

**Revision direction:**  
Show the activity of arranging a sequence rather than presenting an unsolved sequence directly to the child.

The sequence may be partially visible, but the illustration should not require a correct answer.

---

## 6. K68-THINK-05 — REVISE

**Current construct:** Logical integration interest.

**Risk:**  
Several clues leading to one hidden rule can easily become an IQ-style or school-style reasoning problem.

This is the highest ability-contamination risk among the Required Think illustrations.

**Preserve:**  
Interest in combining clues and discovering logical relationships.

**Revision direction:**  
Show a child examining several clue cards, shapes, or relationships as an activity.

Do not make the child viewing the assessment solve the hidden rule.

Avoid:

- missing-answer boxes;
- question marks demanding a solution;
- multiple-choice puzzle answers;
- right/wrong indicators.

## 8. Ability-Contamination Rule

A Kids Interest illustration must never silently become an aptitude item.

The distinction is:

### Correct

Show a child participating in a logical, creative, investigative, practical, social, or physical activity.

Ask whether the user would enjoy trying that activity.

### Incorrect

Present the user with a puzzle, construction problem, visual search task, or reasoning problem that must be successfully solved before they can answer.

The target construct is:

**Interest in the activity.**

It is not:

**Performance on the activity.**

---

## 9. Accessibility Rule

Accessibility review applies to both wording and illustration.

Do not infer low interest from difficulty involving:

- mobility;
- hand dexterity;
- fine visual discrimination;
- hearing;
- speech;
- reading;
- previous familiarity.

Whenever possible, the illustration should depict the activity while allowing multiple valid participation methods.
