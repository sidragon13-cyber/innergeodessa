## 2. Construct Match

The image clearly represents a child engaged in arranging and organising visual elements.

The activity communicates:

- sequencing;
- ordering;
- visual organisation;
- structured arrangement.

The image does not require the viewer to solve the sequence.

Therefore the intended construct remains:

**interest in sequencing activities**

rather than:

**sequencing ability**.

---

## 3. Improvement From V1

Compared with V1:

- the plant lifecycle was removed;
- factual chronology was removed;
- science knowledge is no longer required;
- no natural correct sequence is presented;
- the activity is visually neutral;
- the child is shown participating rather than the viewer being tested.

The primary V1 contamination issue was resolved.

---

## 4. Ability Boundary

The viewer is not asked to:

- identify the next card;
- fill a missing slot;
- select a correct answer;
- recognise a factual sequence;
- demonstrate reasoning performance.

The image illustrates the activity.

It does not turn the assessment into an aptitude task.

---

## 5. Prior-Knowledge Neutrality

The visual elements use simple generic shapes.

Understanding the scene does not require:

- science knowledge;
- school curriculum exposure;
- numerical knowledge;
- reading;
- specialised vocabulary.
