# InnerGeo Kids — Ages 6–8 Required Visual Concepts V1.0

**Status:** Visual Concept Draft  
**Scope:** Required Illustrations Only  
**Required Items:** 14  
**Production Illustration Approved:** No

---

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the first visual concept for each Ages 6–8 item classified as **Required**.

The purpose is not to create attractive artwork.

The purpose is to define a visual representation that:

- improves comprehension;
- matches the intended construct;
- reduces reading burden;
- avoids unnecessary resource signals;
- avoids social-status cues;
- avoids gender or cultural stereotypes;
- supports accessibility;
- does not make one activity artificially more attractive.

Every concept below still requires content-match, bias, accessibility, and visual-balance review before image production.

# CREATE

## K68-CREATE-01 — Original Visual Expression

**Item concept:** Making a picture in the child's own style.

**Primary visual action:**  
A child is shaping an original visual idea using one or more simple methods such as arranging large shapes, selecting forms, moving visual elements, or drawing.

**Scene:**  
Plain table or accessible working surface with minimal background.

**Key visual requirement:**  
The image should emphasise choosing and shaping an original idea rather than conventional drawing skill or fine-motor performance.

**Do not show:**

- professional art studio;
- expensive art supplies;
- unusually beautiful finished artwork;
- adult praise;
- prizes or stars;
- famous characters;
- one gender repeatedly associated with art.

**Accessibility note:**  
Show more than one valid way to create. Do not imply that hand drawing or fine finger control is required for creative participation.

**Bias risk:**  
High visual-attractiveness risk. Keep colours and detail comparable with other domain illustrations.

---

## K68-CREATE-03 — Visual / Design Choice

**Item concept:** Choosing how an ordinary object should look.

**Primary visual action:**  
A child is deciding between simple shapes, patterns, or arrangements for a plain everyday object such as a card, box, or simple container.

**Scene:**  
Neutral low-resource making area.

**Key visual requirement:**  
Focus on choosing visual form rather than decorating an expensive object.

**Do not show:**

- branded products;
- luxury objects;
- highly polished finished design;
- professional design software;
- fashion-status cues.

**Accessibility note:**  
Design choice can be indicated through pointing, arranging, selecting, or another accessible interaction.

**Bias risk:**  
Do not make one design option visibly more attractive than another.

# DISCOVER

## K68-DISCOVER-03 — Observation and Evidence Seeking

**Item concept:** Looking closely at something unusual to find small clues.

**Primary visual action:**  
A child is examining several clear features or clues on an ordinary object, natural item, or simple mechanism.

**Scene:**  
Simple everyday environment.

**Key visual requirement:**  
The activity must look like observation and evidence seeking without requiring the viewer to detect tiny or hidden details.

**Do not show:**

- expensive microscope;
- laboratory;
- dramatic glowing clue;
- detective costume;
- treasure;
- fantasy effects.

**Accessibility note:**  
Clues should be large and visually clear enough to support comprehension without testing fine visual discrimination.

**Bias risk:**  
Avoid making nature imagery inherently more beautiful than other domains.

---

## K68-DISCOVER-05 — Comparing Simple Explanations

**Item concept:** Two possible reasons for an event, with simple clues that help compare them.

**Primary visual action:**  
A neutral event is shown together with two possible causes and two or three simple clues.

**Example visual scenario:**  
A small plant has bent over.

Possible explanation A:
wind affected it.

Possible explanation B:
an object pressed against it.

Simple visual clues help the child understand that there are two possibilities to investigate.

**Key visual requirement:**  
The illustration should clarify “two possible reasons + clues” without turning the item into an actual scored reasoning puzzle.

**Do not show:**

- obvious correct answer;
- mystery/crime framing;
- dangerous event;
- moral blame;
- distressed characters;
- highly complex causal chains.

**Accessibility note:**  
Use large, simple visual differences and minimal background detail.

**Bias risk:**  
The illustration must support comprehension but must not test whether the child can solve the pictured mystery.

# BUILD

## K68-BUILD-01 — Assembly Interest

**Item concept:** Figuring out how several pieces could fit together.

**Primary visual action:**  
Several simple generic pieces are being joined into a basic object or structure.

**Scene:**  
Neutral work surface.

**Key visual requirement:**  
Show the process of fitting pieces together.

**Do not show:**

- LEGO;
- branded construction toys;
- robot kits;
- complex mechanical models;
- completed impressive structure.

**Accessibility note:**  
Do not make fine finger control the visual definition of success. Pieces should be large and manipulable in multiple ways.

**Bias risk:**  
Avoid gender-coded engineering imagery.

---

## K68-BUILD-02 — Construction Interest

**Item concept:** Building a bridge, tower, or shelter using ordinary materials.

**Primary visual action:**  
A child is constructing one simple structure using cardboard, paper tubes, sticks, blocks, or similar low-resource materials.

**Scene:**  
Simple home/classroom-neutral setting.

**Key visual requirement:**  
Construction process must be clearer than the final result.

**Do not show:**

- large professional building;
- power tools;
- expensive kits;
- competition;
- trophy;
- highly advanced structure.

**Accessibility note:**  
Materials should be large and simple, allowing multiple participation methods.

**Bias risk:**  
Avoid associating construction exclusively with boys.

---

## K68-BUILD-05 — Accessible Material Transformation

**Item concept:** Moving, joining, or changing materials to create something useful.

**Primary visual action:**  
A child arranges and joins several ordinary materials into a simple functional object.

**Possible example:**  
Cardboard and simple pieces becoming a holder, container, ramp, or shelter.

**Key visual requirement:**  
The action should represent transformation rather than artistic decoration.

**Do not show:**

- only hand-dependent fine-motor work;
- sharp tools;
- specialist equipment;
- expensive materials;
- finished object that looks professionally manufactured.

**Accessibility note:**  
The visual concept must allow different ways of moving or arranging materials.

**Bias risk:**  
Keep this visually distinct from Create by emphasising function rather than appearance.

---

## K68-BUILD-06 — Build-Test Cycle

**Item concept:** Testing something after building it to see whether it works.

**Primary visual action:**  
A child tests a simple object they have just constructed.

**Possible example:**  
A small ball rolls down a simple homemade ramp.

**Scene:**  
Low-resource neutral setting.

**Key visual requirement:**  
Clearly show:

Build
→
Test

without turning the scene into Discover or Think.

**Do not show:**

- successful celebration;
- failure/frustration;
- complex engineering;
- electronic equipment;
- competition.

**Accessibility note:**  
Testing should be possible using large visible movement or object behaviour.

**Bias risk:**  
The outcome should remain neutral; the interest is in testing, not winning.

# THINK

## K68-THINK-01 — Pattern Reasoning Interest

**Item concept:** Finding the rule in a simple pattern.

**Primary visual action:**  
A child is arranging or examining a few large pattern pieces as an activity, with the pattern concept already visually understandable.

**Scene:**  
No character required unless needed for consistency.

**Key visual requirement:**  
Communicate interest in pattern-based reasoning without presenting an unfinished puzzle that the viewer must solve.

**Do not show:**

- difficult puzzle;
- school examination;
- timer;
- score;
- “correct answer” indicator;
- advanced mathematics.

**Accessibility note:**  
Use large shapes and redundant cues such as shape and position. Do not rely only on colour.

**Bias risk:**  
The image is explanatory, not a scored IQ-style problem.

---

## K68-THINK-02 — Sequencing Interest

**Item concept:** Working out which piece should come next.

**Primary visual action:**  
A child is arranging a simple sequence of large objects or symbols, with the activity shown in progress rather than as a missing-answer task.

**Scene:**  
Minimal background.

**Key visual requirement:**  
Show sequencing as an enjoyable activity without asking the viewer to identify the correct next piece.

**Do not show:**

- difficult missing-piece puzzle;
- timer;
- right/wrong marks;
- school grades;
- competitive environment.

**Accessibility note:**  
Use large distinct forms and avoid colour-only sequencing or small-detail discrimination.

**Bias risk:**  
The illustration should represent the activity the child might enjoy, rather than requiring correct completion before answering.

---

## K68-THINK-03 — Classification Interest

**Item concept:** Sorting objects using a discovered rule.

**Primary visual action:**  
Several simple objects are being placed into two or three groups according to a visible characteristic.

**Possible examples:**

- shape;
- size;
- pattern;
- object type.

**Key visual requirement:**  
Show sorting/classifying as an activity.

**Do not show:**

- academic worksheet;
- complex categories;
- right/wrong symbols;
- adult teacher evaluation.

**Accessibility note:**  
Use more than colour as the classification signal.

**Bias risk:**  
Do not make one category visually more desirable.

---

## K68-THINK-05 — Logical Integration Interest

**Item concept:** Using several clues to find one hidden rule.

**Primary visual action:**  
A child is examining several simple clue cards, shapes, or relationships and exploring how they connect.

**Scene:**  
Minimal.

**Key visual requirement:**  
Show the activity of combining clues and looking for relationships without requiring the viewer to solve a hidden rule.

**Do not show:**

- complex mathematical notation;
- coded symbols requiring prior learning;
- “genius” imagery;
- reward effects;
- competitive puzzle framing.

**Accessibility note:**  
Use large, simple visual elements and avoid symbols that require prior academic knowledge.

**Bias risk:**  
Keep difficulty very low because this item measures interest in logical activity, not logical ability.

---

# MOVE

## K68-MOVE-02 — Movement-Based Exploration

**Item concept:** Moving in one's own way through different places or stations.

**Primary visual action:**  
Several children participate in the same exploration route using different valid forms of movement.

**Possible scene:**  
A simple activity path with several stations.

**Key visual requirement:**  
The visual should communicate that there is no single correct physical movement.

**Do not show:**

- race;
- stopwatch;
- winner;
- athletic competition;
- jumping requirement;
- speed comparison.

**Accessibility note:**  
This is a priority accessibility illustration. Different mobility methods should appear equally normal and equally involved in the activity.

**Bias risk:**  
Avoid presenting disability as special, inspirational, or separate from ordinary participation.

---

## K68-MOVE-05 — Physical / Environmental Exploration

**Item concept:** Exploring how different places feel while moving through them.

**Primary visual action:**  
A child or small group moves through two or three simple environmental zones and notices differences.

**Possible environments:**

- open path;
- shaded area;
- grass/natural area;
- textured surface;
- simple indoor/outdoor transition.

**Key visual requirement:**  
The emphasis is active environmental exploration rather than travel, sport, or adventure prestige.

**Do not show:**

- exotic travel;
- mountain expedition;
- expensive outdoor gear;
- athletic performance;
- dangerous terrain.

**Accessibility note:**  
The environment should allow different mobility methods.

**Bias risk:**  
Outdoor imagery should not be dramatically more exciting or colourful than other domain illustrations.

# 3. Shared Visual Production Rules

All 14 concepts must use the same controlled visual system.

Maintain comparable:

- composition;
- image dimensions;
- character scale;
- colour intensity;
- facial emotion;
- background complexity;
- visual energy;
- level of detail.

The art style must not change by domain.
