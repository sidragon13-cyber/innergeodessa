# InnerGeo Kids — Interest Domain & Item Blueprint V1.0

**Status:** Draft Professional Blueprint  
**Parent Framework:** kids-assessment-framework-v1.md  
**Scope:** Ages 6–8 and 9–12  
**Purpose:** Define what each Kids interest domain measures, what it must not measure, major contamination risks, and requirements for future item development.

---

## 1. Core Measurement Rule

Every scored item must answer one primary question:

> Is the child currently attracted to exploring this kind of activity?

The item should not primarily measure:

- previous opportunity
- family resources
- school quality
- academic attainment
- confidence
- obedience
- intelligence
- popularity
- adult approval
- occupational knowledge
- access to specialist equipment
- prior training

Interest is the target construct.

Previous exposure is a possible confound.

Ability is a separate construct.

---

## 2. Eight-Domain Architecture

The provisional Kids V1 model contains:

1. Create
2. Discover
3. Build
4. Think
5. Connect
6. Lead
7. Move
8. Express

The domains are not rankings of value.

A child may show:

- one clear interest
- several combined interests
- broad interests
- changing interests
- weakly differentiated interests

All are valid outcomes.

---

# DOMAIN 1 — CREATE

## 3. Construct Definition

Create measures attraction to producing something original through imagination, visual form, sound, style, design, or creative transformation.

Core question:

> Does the child enjoy making something that can be shaped by their own ideas?

### Includes

- drawing
- visual design
- colour and form
- imaginative making
- music creation
- creative arrangement
- inventing characters or worlds
- transforming ordinary material creatively

### Does Not Measure

- artistic talent
- drawing skill
- expensive art exposure
- aesthetic sophistication
- academic art achievement
- creativity as a global intelligence trait

### Common Confounds

- access to art materials
- private lessons
- parental praise
- familiarity with digital design tools
- gender-coded presentation
- visual attractiveness of the assessment image

### Age 6–8 Item Direction

Use visible, simple activities:

- making a picture
- inventing a creature
- decorating an object
- choosing how something should look
- making a rhythm

Avoid occupational language such as:

- designer
- illustrator
- architect
- creative director

### Age 9–12 Item Direction

May include:

- developing visual style
- creating original media
- redesigning an object
- combining ideas in a new way
- producing a creative project

### Visual Control

Do not make Create illustrations more colourful or attractive than competing domains.

---

# DOMAIN 2 — DISCOVER

## 4. Construct Definition

Discover measures attraction to investigating questions, observing evidence, experimenting, comparing explanations, and understanding why something happens.

Core question:

> Does the child enjoy finding out how or why something works?

### Includes

- experiments
- observation
- investigation
- asking why
- testing explanations
- exploring nature
- discovering patterns in evidence
- understanding systems

### Does Not Measure

- science grades
- intelligence
- mathematical ability
- school achievement
- factual knowledge
- access to laboratories
- scientific vocabulary

### Common Confounds

- school science exposure
- access to museums
- technology access
- reading level
- prior knowledge
- adult praise for being "smart"

### Age 6–8 Item Direction

Use curiosity-based situations:

- finding out why something changed
- testing what happens next
- looking closely at something unusual
- comparing two possible explanations

### Age 9–12 Item Direction

May include:

- simple investigation planning
- comparing evidence
- testing explanations
- exploring how natural, technological, or human systems work

### Visual Control

Avoid laboratory imagery being automatically more impressive than ordinary investigation.

Discovery can occur with simple everyday objects.

---

# DOMAIN 3 — BUILD

## 5. Construct Definition

Build measures attraction to hands-on construction, assembly, repair, materials, tools, physical systems, and practical problem solving.

Core question:

> Does the child enjoy making physical things work?

### Includes

- assembling
- constructing
- repairing
- manipulating materials
- practical making
- physical problem solving
- testing physical objects
- improving how something works

### Does Not Measure

- mechanical ability
- strength
- fine motor ability
- access to tools
- ownership of construction toys
- engineering aptitude

### Common Confounds

- access to LEGO or branded toys
- workshop exposure
- gender stereotypes
- physical disability
- household resources
- parental occupation

### Age 6–8 Item Direction

Use ordinary materials:

- blocks
- paper
- cardboard
- sticks
- pieces
- simple objects

Example concept:

> Would you enjoy figuring out how several pieces could fit together?

### Age 9–12 Item Direction

May include:

- designing a simple structure
- repairing an ordinary object
- improving a physical design
- testing whether a constructed object works

### Visual Control

Do not use expensive robotics kits, branded construction toys, or professional workshops as the default representation.

---

# DOMAIN 4 — THINK

## 6. Construct Definition

Think measures attraction to structured reasoning, logical relationships, puzzles, strategy, classification, number patterns, and mentally solving constrained problems.

Core question:

> Does the child enjoy problems where relationships, rules, or patterns need to be worked out?

### Includes

- logic puzzles
- strategy
- sequencing
- number relationships
- classification
- pattern recognition
- rule-based problem solving
- planning moves

### Does Not Measure

- IQ
- mathematics achievement
- school grades
- processing speed
- giftedness
- memory capacity

### Common Confounds

- mathematics confidence
- school performance
- tutoring
- competition experience
- test anxiety
- adult praise for correct answers

### Age 6–8 Item Direction

Focus on enjoyment rather than correctness:

- spotting a pattern
- solving a simple puzzle
- arranging things by a rule
- planning several moves

### Age 9–12 Item Direction

May include:

- strategy problems
- logical comparisons
- classification challenges
- numerical patterns
- planning under rules

### Item Rule

Never ask:

> Are you good at puzzles?

Ask:

> Would you enjoy working out the rule behind a puzzle?

---

# DOMAIN 5 — CONNECT

## 7. Construct Definition

Connect measures attraction to helping, teaching, listening, explaining, cooperating, supporting, and contributing to another person's development or wellbeing.

Core question:

> Does the child enjoy activities where understanding or helping another person matters?

### Includes

- helping
- teaching
- listening
- explaining
- cooperation
- supporting
- mentoring-like behaviour
- group wellbeing

### Does Not Measure

- kindness
- morality
- obedience
- popularity
- empathy ability
- emotional maturity
- number of friends

### Common Confounds

- social desirability
- cultural expectations
- gender expectations
- family caregiving roles
- popularity
- adult approval

### Age 6–8 Item Direction

Use neutral situations:

- showing another child how something works
- helping someone understand
- working together
- listening when someone needs help

Avoid moral framing.

Poor:

> Would you help a sad child?

This creates an obvious socially desirable answer.

Better:

> Would you enjoy showing someone how to do something you understand?

### Age 9–12 Item Direction

May include:

- explaining
- peer support
- cooperative problem solving
- helping groups work together
- guiding someone through a learning task

---

# DOMAIN 6 — LEAD

## 8. Construct Definition

Lead measures attraction to initiating action, organising people or activities, influencing decisions, setting direction, presenting ideas, and taking responsibility for shared goals.

Core question:

> Does the child enjoy helping an activity move from idea to coordinated action?

### Includes

- initiative
- organising
- presenting ideas
- decision-making
- persuading
- coordinating
- starting projects
- shared goal leadership

### Does Not Measure

- dominance
- popularity
- confidence
- extroversion
- aggression
- social status
- superior ability

### Common Confounds

- speaking confidence
- classroom hierarchy
- language fluency
- cultural expectations about authority
- gender stereotypes
- popularity

### Age 6–8 Item Direction

Avoid business or authority language.

Use:

- choosing how a group activity begins
- organising a game
- suggesting a plan
- helping a group decide what to do

### Age 9–12 Item Direction

May include:

- starting a project
- presenting an idea
- coordinating roles
- negotiating different preferences
- deciding how to reach a shared goal

### Item Rule

Do not equate leadership with being loud or being "the boss."

---

# DOMAIN 7 — MOVE

## 9. Construct Definition

Move measures attraction to active physical engagement, outdoor exploration, nature, animals, movement, and learning through bodily participation.

Core question:

> Does the child enjoy exploring through movement and active contact with the physical or natural world?

### Includes

- physical movement
- outdoor activity
- nature exploration
- animals
- active games
- environmental interaction
- bodily exploration
- movement-based participation

### Does Not Measure

- athletic ability
- physical fitness
- body type
- sports achievement
- coordination
- health status

### Common Confounds

- access to sports facilities
- physical disability
- neighbourhood safety
- climate
- family resources
- gendered sport expectations
- previous coaching

### Age 6–8 Item Direction

Use broad activity:

- exploring outdoors
- moving through an obstacle course
- observing animals outside
- active discovery games

### Age 9–12 Item Direction

May include:

- outdoor exploration
- environmental activity
- physical challenge
- movement-based teamwork
- working with animals or natural settings

### Accessibility Rule

Move must not imply that physical disability equals low interest in active or natural exploration.

Alternative accessible representations must be considered during illustration review.

---

# DOMAIN 8 — EXPRESS

## 10. Construct Definition

Express measures attraction to communicating ideas through language, storytelling, speaking, writing, presentation, performance, or explanation.

Core question:

> Does the child enjoy turning thoughts or experiences into something other people can understand?

### Includes

- storytelling
- speaking
- writing
- presenting
- explaining ideas
- performance
- verbal expression
- communicating a message

### Does Not Measure

- language proficiency
- reading level
- writing ability
- extroversion
- confidence
- vocabulary size
- school language grades

### Common Confounds

- first/second language status
- literacy
- speech differences
- cultural norms about speaking
- classroom confidence
- access to books or performance training

### Age 6–8 Item Direction

Use low-literacy options:

- telling a story
- explaining an idea
- acting out something
- sharing what happened

### Age 9–12 Item Direction

May include:

- writing
- presentations
- storytelling
- podcast/video-style communication without brand references
- explaining ideas to an audience

### Accessibility Rule

Expression can occur through different communication modes.

Do not define verbal fluency as the only valid form.

# 11. Domain Separation Rules

Several domains may overlap in real activities.

The item bank must distinguish the underlying attraction.

Example:

## Create vs Express

Create:

> I want to invent something original.

Express:

> I want to communicate an idea to other people.

## Discover vs Think

Discover:

> I want to investigate what is happening.

Think:

> I want to solve the logical structure of the problem.

## Build vs Move

Build:

> I want to make or repair the physical object.

Move:

> I want to participate actively in the physical or natural activity.

## Connect vs Lead

Connect:

> I want to support or help people.

Lead:

> I want to organise action toward a shared goal.

Items that cannot clearly distinguish their primary domain should be revised or removed.

---

# 12. Cross-Domain Activity Rule

Real activities can represent multiple interests.

For example:

Building a wildlife shelter could involve:

- Build
- Move
- Discover
- Connect

The assessment should not assign an activity to multiple scored domains merely because several interpretations are possible.

Each scored item needs one defined target construct.

Cross-domain combinations belong in result interpretation, not ambiguous item scoring.

# 13. Candidate Item Count

Before pilot testing:

## Ages 6–8

Target candidate pool:

- 6–8 candidate items per domain

Total candidate pool:

- approximately 48–64 items

Possible final scored form:

- approximately 24–32 items

Final count remains provisional.

## Ages 9–12

Target candidate pool:

- 8–10 candidate items per domain

Total candidate pool:

- approximately 64–80 items

Possible final scored form:

- approximately 36–48 items

Final count remains provisional.

Candidate pools should be larger than release forms so weak or biased items can be removed without creating construct gaps.

---

# 14. Context Balance

Each domain should avoid using only one context.

Candidate items should be distributed across contexts such as:

- home
- school-like learning
- free time
- outdoors
- group activity
- individual activity
- making
- exploration
- communication
- problem solving

Do not assume all children have equal access to any one setting.

---

# 15. Social Desirability Control

Avoid items where one answer obviously appears more:

- kind
- intelligent
- responsible
- brave
- creative
- hardworking
- socially approved

The assessment measures preference.

It should not reward the child for guessing which activity adults prefer.

---

# 16. Exposure Bias Control

For every candidate item ask:

1. Does understanding this item require previous experience?
2. Does enjoying it require expensive resources?
3. Does it assume access to a particular institution?
4. Does it depend on a parent's occupation?
5. Does it use adult career terminology?
6. Does familiarity make one response easier to choose?
7. Can the same interest be represented using an ordinary activity?

If exposure dependency is unnecessary, rewrite the item.

---

# 17. Bilingual Equivalence

English and Chinese items must measure the same underlying interest.

Translation review must check:

- activity meaning
- intensity
- age appropriateness
- cultural assumptions
- emotional tone
- difficulty
- social desirability

Do not translate literally when literal translation changes the child-facing meaning.

---

# 18. Illustration Review

Every illustrated item requires review for:

- domain clarity
- attractiveness balance
- colour intensity
- character emotion
- action level
- background complexity
- resource signalling
- gender representation
- cultural representation
- accessibility
- accidental clues about the "better" choice

The image must support comprehension.

It must not become the primary source of preference bias.

---

# 19. Parent Interference Control

Before assessment, parents should receive:

> Help your child understand what the activity means, but do not tell them which answer is better or which activity you want them to choose.

When a child asks:

> Which one should I pick?

Recommended parent response:

> Pick the one you would most like to try.

---

# 20. Scoring Design Requirements

Scoring has not yet been frozen.

Future scoring must support:

- all eight domains
- relative domain strength
- score gaps
- close-domain detection
- broad-interest detection
- emerging-interest detection
- no forced Top 3 when differentiation is weak

The scoring system must not reproduce the adult Career deterministic tie-ranking problem.

A completely or substantially tied profile must remain visibly broad or undifferentiated.

---

# 21. Result Interpretation Principle

The assessment should convert:

Responses
→
Interest Signals
→
Domain Pattern
→
Exploration Suggestions
→
Real-World Activities
→
Observation
→
Updated Understanding

It should not convert:

Responses
→
Permanent Child Label
→
Future Career Prediction

---

# 22. Candidate Item Review Record

Every future candidate item should eventually record:

- item ID
- age band
- target domain
- item format
- English wording
- Chinese wording
- intended construct
- context
- exposure dependency
- cultural dependency
- social desirability risk
- accessibility risk
- visual requirement
- reviewer status
- content version
- pilot status

No candidate item should enter the production bank without this traceability.

---

# 23. Release Principle

A good Kids item is not simply:

> Easy for a child to understand.

It must also be:

> Clear about what it measures.

> Fair across different childhood environments.

> Difficult to answer merely by guessing what adults approve of.

> Appropriate to the child's developmental stage.

> Useful for deciding what to explore next.
