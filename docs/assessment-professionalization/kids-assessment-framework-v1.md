# InnerGeo Kids — Interest Discovery Framework V1.0

**Status:** Design Framework  
**Target:** Children aged 6–12 with parent or guardian support  
**Product Type:** Interest discovery and guided exploration  
**Not:** Personality typing, aptitude testing, intelligence testing, talent diagnosis, or career prediction

---

## 1. Product Purpose

InnerGeo Kids helps children and parents notice emerging patterns of curiosity, enjoyment, participation, and willingness to explore different kinds of activities.

The assessment does not determine what a child should become.

Its purpose is:

Discover
→
Explore
→
Experiment
→
Observe
→
Develop

Core principle:

> We do not decide a child's future. We help families notice what may be worth exploring next.

---

## 2. What the Assessment Measures

Kids Interest Discovery measures current patterns of:

- curiosity
- activity preference
- willingness to participate
- enjoyment
- voluntary engagement
- preferred ways of exploring
- repeated attraction to activity types

It does not directly measure:

- intelligence
- academic achievement
- fixed talent
- aptitude
- personality type
- future occupation
- professional ability
- future success

Interest should be interpreted as a signal for exploration, not proof of ability.

---

## 3. Age Architecture

### Age 6–8 — Early Explorer

Primary design:

- image-first
- minimal reading
- simple activity choices
- concrete situations
- short sessions
- parent-assisted comprehension
- no occupational terminology

Target completion time:

8–10 minutes.

Initial candidate range:

24–32 scored items.

Final item count must be determined after item review and pilot testing.

### Age 9–12 — Growing Explorer

Primary design:

- image + short text
- activity scenarios
- preference intensity
- curiosity
- persistence
- independent versus social exploration
- slightly broader contexts

Target completion time:

12–15 minutes.

Initial candidate range:

36–48 scored items.

Final item count must be determined after item review and pilot testing.

## 4. Interest Domain Model

The first framework uses eight child-facing interest domains.

Names remain provisional until content review.

### 1. Create

Creative expression, art, imagination, design, music, visual communication, storytelling, and making something original.

### 2. Discover

Science, investigation, observation, experiments, asking why, finding patterns, and understanding how things work.

### 3. Build

Making, assembling, repairing, constructing, using materials, practical experimentation, and hands-on problem solving.

### 4. Think

Numbers, logic, puzzles, strategy, classification, patterns, structured problem solving, and reasoning challenges.

### 5. Connect

Helping, teaching, listening, cooperation, caring, explaining, and supporting other people.

### 6. Lead

Starting activities, organising groups, persuading, planning shared goals, making decisions, and taking initiative.

### 7. Move

Physical activity, nature, outdoor exploration, movement, animals, environmental interaction, and active participation.

### 8. Express

Language, speaking, writing, presenting, storytelling, communication, performance, and sharing ideas.

---

## 5. Relationship to RIASEC

RIASEC may be used internally as one theoretical reference.

Kids must not simply receive adult RIASEC labels.

The child-facing model must remain developmentally appropriate.

Possible backend theoretical relationships may include:

- Build / Move → Realistic-related activities
- Discover / Think → Investigative-related activities
- Create / Express → Artistic-related activities
- Connect → Social-related activities
- Lead → Enterprising-related activities
- structured organisation elements → Conventional-related activities

These relationships are conceptual references, not one-to-one equivalence.

The Kids system requires independent validation.

## 6. Item Design Principle

Items should measure attraction to an activity rather than previous access to it.

Poor:

> Do you like visiting science museums?

Better:

> Would you enjoy exploring why an experiment produced a surprising result?

Poor:

> Do you enjoy LEGO?

Better:

> Would you enjoy building something from many small pieces?

Poor:

> Do you like taking art classes?

Better:

> Would you enjoy creating a picture in your own style?

Avoid unnecessary dependence on:

- brands
- expensive toys
- private lessons
- museums
- travel
- elite schools
- home technology
- specialised equipment
- previous extracurricular exposure

---

## 7. Item Formats

Possible formats include:

### Activity Preference

Which activity would you rather try?

### Visual Choice

Choose the picture showing the activity you would most like to do.

### Situational Choice

If you had free time, which activity sounds most interesting?

### Interest Intensity

How much would you like to try this?

### Persistence

Would you want to keep working on this if it became difficult?

Persistence items should be used cautiously because persistence can reflect ability, confidence, prior experience, or adult expectations.

---

## 8. Response Design

Children should not receive adult psychological scales.

Candidate response systems:

### Ages 6–8

Visual three-point scale:

- Not for me
- Maybe
- I want to try

or equivalent child-friendly visual responses.

### Ages 9–12

Four- or five-level interest scale may be considered.

Avoid language implying:

- good/bad
- smart/not smart
- talented/not talented
- successful/unsuccessful

---

## 9. Visual Standard

Images must measure interest rather than illustration attractiveness.

All assessment illustrations should maintain comparable:

- composition complexity
- colour intensity
- visual energy
- character size
- background detail
- action intensity
- image quality

Avoid one option being visually more exciting than another.

Images must avoid:

- commercial brands
- copyrighted characters
- gender stereotypes
- ethnic stereotypes
- occupational stereotypes
- luxury-resource signalling

Representation should be diverse without making identity itself the measured variable.

---

## 10. Parent Role

The default relationship is:

Parent Account
→
Child Profile
→
Kids Assessment

Parent guidance:

> Please help your child understand the question, but allow them to choose their own answer.

Parents should not:

- answer on behalf of the child
- coach toward preferred activities
- explain which answer appears more desirable
- use the result to pressure the child into a fixed direction

---

## 11. Child Result Language

Never describe a child as:

- weak
- low ability
- not talented
- unsuitable
- incapable
- bad at something
- destined for a career

Preferred language:

- currently drawn to
- enjoys exploring
- frequently selected
- emerging interest
- worth trying
- may enjoy
- appears curious about
- less frequently selected at this stage

Lower-ranked domains are not weaknesses.

---

## 12. Result Structure

The primary result should be an:

# Interest Map

Not a winner/loser scorecard.

Possible presentation:

- all eight domains visible
- strongest current signals highlighted
- Top 3 exploration areas
- combinations
- broad-interest profile when several domains are close
- emerging-interest profile when signals are weak or mixed

Do not force a Top 3 hierarchy when differentiation is insufficient.

---

## 13. Low-Differentiation Handling

Kids must not repeat the adult Career fixed-tie problem.

Possible result states include:

### Clear Exploration Pattern

Several domains show consistent stronger preference.

### Blended Interest Pattern

Multiple domains are close and may work together.

### Broad Explorer

Many domains are similarly attractive.

### Emerging Explorer

Preference signals are currently weak, inconsistent, or still developing.

A broad profile is a valid result.

It must not be treated as test failure.

---

## 14. Free Result

Possible free result:

- Interest Map
- Top 3 exploration areas when sufficiently differentiated
- short description of each
- one activity idea per highlighted area
- parent explanation that interest is not ability

Free results should still be useful.

## 16. Three-Month Exploration Model

The report should convert results into experimentation.

### Month 1 — Explore

Choose activities from the leading domains.

Observe:

- voluntary engagement
- curiosity
- emotional response
- desire to continue

### Month 2 — Experiment

Introduce slightly more difficult activities.

Observe:

- persistence
- learning behaviour
- frustration response
- willingness to return

### Month 3 — Reflect

Parent and child review:

- What was enjoyable?
- What became more interesting with practice?
- What looked exciting but became boring?
- What did the child choose without prompting?
- What should be explored next?

The objective is not to confirm the test.

The objective is to collect better real-world evidence.

---

## 17. Interest vs Ability

The system must repeatedly distinguish:

Interest
=
What the child is attracted to exploring.

Ability
=
What the child can currently do.

Skill
=
What has been learned through practice.

Strength
=
A useful pattern that may emerge through interest, ability, practice, context, and feedback.

Fit
=
How well the child and an activity or environment work together.

High interest does not guarantee high ability.

Low current ability does not invalidate an interest.

---

## 18. Privacy and Data Minimization

V1 should collect only information necessary to deliver the service.

Preferred Child Profile:

- nickname
- age band
- language
- assessment result
- assessment date

Avoid collecting unless required for a clearly defined future function:

- exact home address
- school name
- personal phone number
- child email
- child photograph
- unnecessary exact birth date
- unnecessary identity information

Parent account remains the primary account.

---

## 19. Safety and Ethics

Kids Interest Discovery must not be used for:

- school admission
- academic streaming
- psychological diagnosis
- intelligence classification
- employment prediction
- talent certification
- exclusion from activities
- permanent career decisions

The system must avoid deterministic statements about a child's future.

---

## 20. Longitudinal Development

Future versions may allow repeated assessments.

Potential feature:

# Interest Development Timeline

It could show:

- previous Interest Maps
- changing domain signals
- activities tried
- parent observations
- emerging interests
- persistent interests

Change should be presented as normal development, not inconsistency.

This is not required for V1.

---

## 21. Professional Validation Levels

### Level 1 — Engineering Validated

Structure, scoring, rendering, storage, contracts, and versioning pass.

### Level 2 — Professionally Reviewed

Items, age appropriateness, visual neutrality, bias, bilingual meaning, interpretation, report, and parent guidance reviewed.

### Level 3 — Empirically / Psychometrically Evaluated

Requires real child sample evidence appropriate to the instrument.

InnerGeo must not claim this level before evidence exists.

## 23. Product Principle

Do not ask:

> What will this child become?

Ask:

> What is this child curious enough to explore next?

Do not label early.

Create opportunities to discover.

The result is not the destination.

It is the next experiment.
