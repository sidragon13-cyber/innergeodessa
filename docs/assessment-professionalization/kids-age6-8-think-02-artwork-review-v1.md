## 2. What Works

The image clearly shows a child engaged in arranging a sequence.

The activity is:

- age appropriate;
- easy to understand;
- visually calm;
- consistent with the developing InnerGeo Kids illustration style.

The scene successfully communicates that sequencing is an activity.

---

## 3. Main Problem — Natural Correct Sequence

The cards depict a plant lifecycle:

seed
→
sprout
→
young plant
→
larger plant
→
flower

This creates an objectively meaningful chronological order.

The viewer may therefore interpret the image as:

> Put these cards in the correct order.

This introduces ability and prior-knowledge contamination.

The child may begin evaluating whether the sequence is correct rather than deciding whether sequencing activities are enjoyable.

---

## 4. Prior-Knowledge Risk

Plant growth may depend partly on:

- previous science exposure;
- school knowledge;
- familiarity with life-cycle activities;
- understanding of natural chronology.

The target construct is not science knowledge.

The target construct is:

**interest in arranging and understanding sequences.**

---

## 5. V2 Direction

Keep:

- one child;
- independent 4:3 asset;
- simple table;
- arranging-card activity;
- calm editorial illustration style;
- low background complexity.

Replace:

- plant lifecycle;
- chronological natural process;
- cards with an obvious factual answer.

Use a neutral sequencing activity where the child is visibly organising or arranging items, but the viewer does not need to solve the sequence.

Possible visual direction:

- several simple geometric or everyday-object cards;
- the child is actively moving and arranging them;
- some cards already form a visually coherent arrangement;
- no empty answer slot;
- no arrows;
- no question marks;
- no right/wrong cue;
- no obvious factual chronology.

The image should communicate:

> This child is enjoying arranging things into a sequence.

It should not communicate:

> Solve this sequence.
