## Move from Automatically Filling Gaps to Responsibility Clarification

ISFJ-related preferences may make it natural to notice a gap and:

- help;
- record;
- remind;
- finish;
- maintain the process;
- protect the relationship.

These actions can be valuable in the short term.

Profile B should continue by asking:

> **Does this responsibility actually belong to me?**

Distinguish:

### My Responsibility
I must own this.

### Shared Responsibility
This needs to be shared.

### Support
I can help, but I do not own the final responsibility.

### Not Mine
I should not carry this continuously.

## Move from Harmony to Clear Boundaries

A Feeling preference may make relational stability important, while a Judging preference may make commitments and clear arrangements especially salient.

- say no;

- adjust a boundary without ending the relationship.

Profile B can train:

# 02｜Thinking & Decision Structure

## Information Processing: Move from Facts and Needs to Priority

A Sensing preference may make it easy to notice:

- who needs what;
- which step failed;
- which detail was missed;
- which commitment remains unfinished.

Profile B does not need to do more.

The more useful task is:

> **Decide what matters most.**

Ask:

1. What must be handled now?
2. What can be delegated?
3. What am I doing mainly because I am used to doing it myself?
4. What will affect the long-term result?
5. What needs a system-level solution?

---

## Internal Processing: Independent Responsibility Also Needs Early Communication About Load

An Introversion preference may make it natural to:

- handle something alone first;
- avoid inconveniencing others;
- wait until a problem is clear before speaking.

In collaboration, late communication may mean that others do not know:

- you are overloaded;
- a task is at risk;
- resources are insufficient;
- responsibility needs to be redistributed.

A useful rule is:

> **Early Signal**

Communicate before overload becomes a crisis.

---

## Decision-Making: Caring About People Does Not Require Lowering Standards

A Feeling preference may make you naturally consider:

- the other person’s feelings;
- context;
- relationships;
- fairness.

Profile B should avoid:

> **Blurring standards simply to prevent discomfort.**

Use:

> **Warmth + Clear Standard**

Respect people while keeping expectations clear.

---

## Closure: Commitments Can Be Renegotiated

A Judging preference may make it difficult to change something once you have agreed to it.

In reality:

> **Renegotiating a commitment does not automatically make you unreliable.**

When conditions change, timely communication can be more reliable than forcing yourself to carry an unsustainable commitment.
