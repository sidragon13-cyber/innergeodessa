# 02｜Thinking & Decision Structure

## Information Processing: Move from Pattern Recognition to Evidence Quality

An Intuition preference may make patterns, relationships, trends, and longer-term possibilities easier to notice.

When facing complex information, you may naturally ask:

- Which observations belong to the same underlying problem?
- Which variables determine the overall result?
- How might the current trend develop?
- Is there a higher-level explanation?
- Which changes may create longer-term effects?

This can be a useful information orientation.

For Profile C, however, the next step is not to increase abstraction.

It is to improve:

> **Evidence Quality**

The reliability of a model depends on questions such as:

- Is the data trustworthy?
- Is the sample sufficient?
- Are the assumptions explicit?
- Are there counterexamples?
- Has the model been tested in reality?

A useful process is:

> **Pattern → Evidence → Test → Revision**

This moves judgment beyond simply “seeing the trend” toward a more professional standard of reasoning.

---

## Independent Judgment: Build an External Validation Mechanism

An Introversion preference may make internal integration a natural way of processing information.

For Profile C, there is no need to prove that internal thinking has value.

The more important task is:

> **Build a reliable external validation mechanism.**

For example:

### Peer Review

Ask people with relevant expertise to examine the reasoning.

### User Feedback

Let real users test the judgment.

### Data Check

Use data to test conclusions.

### Pre-Mortem

Assume the plan has failed and identify possible reasons in advance.

### Counter-Argument

Actively search for the strongest opposing view.

These methods help reduce the risk of:

> **An internal model becoming increasingly complete while real-world validation remains insufficient.**

---

## Decision-Making: Move from Logical Coherence to Real-World Feasibility

A Thinking preference may make logic, consistency, standards, and causal reasoning important reference points.

But high-quality decisions cannot answer only:

> “Does this make sense?”

They should also ask:

- Can it actually be executed?
- Will the necessary people be willing to execute it?
- Are the costs realistic?
- Are the resources sufficient?
- Is there enough time?
- Does the organization support it?
- If it fails, is the loss acceptable?

A useful framework is:

> **Reasoning + Feasibility + Acceptance + Feedback**

This moves a decision from “theoretically correct” toward “effective in reality.”

---

## Closure: Build a Decision Rhythm

A Judging preference may make direction and closure feel natural.

Profile C does not require faster decisions.

The more useful development task is:

> **Build a consistent decision rhythm.**

Decisions can be separated into:

### Fast Decisions

Low-cost and reversible.

Move into action quickly.

### Deliberate Decisions

High-cost or difficult to reverse.

Increase evidence, counterevidence, and external validation.

### Review Points

Set review points after the decision.

This preserves the benefits of structure while reducing the risk of becoming overly committed simply because a decision has already been made.
