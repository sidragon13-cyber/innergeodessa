# 02｜Thinking & Decision Structure

## Information Processing: Move from Concrete Experiences to Patterns

A Sensing preference may make it easy to remember:

- a particular interaction;
- a specific problem;
- an environmental detail;
- an actual result.

The Profile B task is:

> **Determine whether these concrete events form a repeating pattern.**

Ask:

1. Has it happened repeatedly?
2. Under what conditions does it happen?
3. Who is affected?
4. Is there a common cause?
5. Is it worth changing the environment or method?

---

## Internal Processing: Independent Thinking Still Needs Timely Externalization

An Introversion preference may make it natural to:

- process things privately first;
- avoid explaining immediately;
- wait until you feel more certain before speaking.

In important relationships and collaboration, communicating too late can prevent others from adjusting in time.

Use:

> **Early Signal, Later Detail**

You can say:

> “There is something I still need to think through, but I already know that some adjustment may be needed.”

You do not need to wait until everything is fully resolved before communicating.

---

## Decision-Making: Values Need Real-World Cost Too

A Feeling preference may make “Does this fit who I am?” especially important.

Profile B should continue by asking:

- What does this choice cost?
- What is the opportunity cost?
- What long-term responsibilities come with it?
- What happens if I do not choose it?
- Does real-world evidence support it?

Use:

> **Values + Evidence + Cost + Commitment**

---

## Closure: Build Stage-Based Choices

A Perceiving preference may make permanent commitments feel restrictive.

Profile B can use:

> **Choose → Try → Review**

Stage-based choices create real-world evidence.
