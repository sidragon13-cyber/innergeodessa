# 01｜Core Personality Profile

## Move from Immediate Fixes to Reusable Problem-Solving Capability

Profile C shows a relatively clear ISTP preference structure.

You may often:

- need some independent space to analyze problems;
- focus on real-world facts, current state, and direct feedback;
- use logic, causality, and efficiency in judgment;
- want room to adjust based on new information;
- notice mechanisms, abnormalities, and inefficient points;
- prefer to judge methods by real-world outcomes.

The most important boundary is:

> **Clear preferences do not mean that technical ability, analytical ability, or problem-solving capability is already mature.**

Capability requires real-world evidence.

---

## Move from “I Can Fix It” to “I Can Build a Method”

Solving a problem once is a result.

Solving problems repeatedly, reliably, and across contexts begins to look more like capability.

Profile C can turn each real problem into:

- Problem Definition;
- Hypothesis;
- Test;
- Root Cause;
- Fix;
- Verification;
- Documentation.

This creates:

> **Problem-Solving System**

rather than dependence on personal experience alone.

---

## Move from Rapid Testing to Experiment Design

A clear Sensing preference may make direct feedback especially useful, while a clear Perceiving preference may make experimentation feel natural.

The next step is:

> **Not more tests, but better tests.**

High-quality experiments need:

- explicit hypotheses;
- controlled variables;
- observable metrics;
- risk boundaries;
- stop conditions;
- recorded outcomes.

This can turn “try and see” into professional experimental practice.

---

## Move from Simple Solutions to Maintainable Solutions

ISTP-related preferences may make you value:

- fewer steps;
- directness;
- effectiveness;
- low complexity.

This can be a useful starting point.

Professional systems also need to consider:

- whether others can understand them;
- whether they can scale;
- whether they can be maintained;
- whether standards are met;
- whether they are safe;
- whether they can be handed over.

Therefore:

> **Simple ≠ Sustainable**
