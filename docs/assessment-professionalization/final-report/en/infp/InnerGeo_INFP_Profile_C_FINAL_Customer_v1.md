# 01｜Core Personality Profile

## Move from Value Identification to Real-World Creation

Profile C shows a relatively clear INFP preference structure.

You may often:

- need independent space to process complex feelings and choices;
- notice possibilities, meaning, and future direction;
- include values, individual differences, and human impact in judgment;
- prefer room for adjustment rather than premature closure;
- pay attention to questions such as “Is this authentic?” and “Is this worth doing?”;
- want work and life to connect with personal values.

The most important boundary is:

> **Clear preferences do not mean that creativity, empathy, artistic ability, or self-understanding is already mature.**

Capability requires real-world evidence.

---

## Move from “This Matters to Me” to “Can I Create Value from It?”

Personal meaning can support sustained investment.

But real-world value also requires:

- capability;

- services;

- feedback from others.

# 02｜Thinking & Decision Structure

## Information Processing: Move from Possibility to Evidence Quality

Clear Intuition may make it natural to:

- imagine future paths;
- connect different concepts;
- notice alternative interpretations;
- construct ideal states.

Profile C should train:

> **Which possibilities deserve confidence?**

Ask:

1. What facts support this?
2. Have I actually experienced it?
3. Is it mainly imagination?
4. What counterexamples exist?
5. How can I test it at low cost?

---

## Internal Processing: Make Personal Experience Communicable

Clear Introversion may mean that important processing happens internally.

If others cannot see the process, they may be unable to:

- understand your needs;
- correct misunderstandings;
- collaborate with you;
- handle conflict early.

Profile C can train:

> **Make inner reasoning visible.**

---

## Decision-Making: Values Need to Enter Together with Real Constraints

Clear Feeling may make value alignment an important long-term decision variable.

Real choices also require questions such as:

- Can I carry the cost?
- Is there another route?
- Do I have the resources for the long term?
- Is this merely short-term discomfort?
- What is the cost of being wrong?

A useful decision frame is:

> **Values + Evidence + Responsibility**

---

## Closure: Use Time-Bounded Commitment to Build Depth

Clear Perceiving may make adjustment and openness natural.

Profile C can use:

> **Commit for a period, then review.**

For example:

- invest in one professional direction for six months;
- complete one full project cycle;
- do not change constantly because of new ideas during the cycle;
- review with evidence at the end.

This allows openness and depth to coexist.
