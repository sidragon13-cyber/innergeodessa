ENFP-related preferences may make starting new projects, relationships, and ideas feel natural.

# 02｜Thinking & Decision Structure

## Information Processing: Move from New Possibilities to Priority

An Intuition preference may make it natural to:

- see new opportunities;
- connect different people and resources;
- imagine alternatives;
- notice new development paths.

Profile B should focus on:

> **Not discovering more, but choosing better.**

Ask:

1. Which problem is most worth solving?
2. Which idea has real-world evidence?
3. Which direction best fits long-term values?
4. Which experiment has the lowest cost?
5. Which opportunity is worth giving up other opportunities for?

---

## External Interaction: Do Not Let Immediate Response Replace Long-Term Judgment

An Extraversion preference may make external feedback especially influential.

Someone showing interest in you, a project receiving positive responses, or a new environment creating excitement can all reinforce a direction.

Profile B should ask:

> **Is this feedback only an immediate reaction, or is it longer-term evidence?**

Longer-term judgment should include:

- time;
- multiple contexts;
- actual outcomes;
- your own sustained investment.

---

## Decision-Making: Values and Feelings Also Need Standards

A Feeling preference may make it natural to ask:

- Do I like it?
- Is it meaningful?
- Does it fit my values?
- How does it affect other people?

Real decisions also require:

- facts;
- cost;
- risk;
- time;
- responsibility;
- opportunity cost.

Use:

> **Values + Evidence + Cost + Commitment**

---

## Closure: Time-Bounded Commitment Is Not the Loss of Freedom

A Perceiving preference may make fixed arrangements feel restrictive.

But time-bounded commitment is not permanent lock-in.

Use:

> **Commit → Review → Adjust**

Commit for a defined period.

Then review.

This allows openness and accumulation to coexist.
