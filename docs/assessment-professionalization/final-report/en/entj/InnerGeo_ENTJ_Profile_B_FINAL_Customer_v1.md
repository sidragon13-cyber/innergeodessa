ENTJ-related preferences may make you quick to notice:

- unclear responsibility;
- inefficient processes;
- delayed decisions;

Forward movement becomes more valuable when it remains correctable.

# 02｜Thinking & Decision Structure

## Information Processing: Strategic Perspective Needs Real Information

An Intuition preference may make you attentive to:

- long-term direction;
- opportunities;
- system impact;
- causal chains;
- future possibilities.

Profile B does not need a larger vision as much as it needs:

> **Better real-world calibration.**

Use questions such as:

1. What facts are currently confirmed?
2. What is still speculation?
3. Which variable matters most?
4. What evidence conflicts with the current view?
5. What information should be gathered next?

---

## External Interaction: Use Discussion Without Letting It Replace Thinking

An Extraversion preference may make meetings, conversation, live feedback, and fast exchange useful for developing judgment.

This can improve information flow.

It can also cause:

- views to form too quickly;
- louder voices to influence direction disproportionately;
- teams to act around one solution too early;
- unspoken dissent to disappear.

For important decisions, use:

### Silent Review
Read and think independently first.

### Open Discussion
Then discuss.

### Dissent Round
Invite opposing views deliberately.

### Decision
Form the current version of the decision.

---

## Decision-Making: Move from “Reasonable” to “Executable”

A Thinking preference may naturally begin with:

> “Is this solution reasonable?”

Profile B should also ask:

- Who will execute it?
- What resources are required?
- Who will be affected?
- Which relationships will change?
- Where is failure most likely?
- Can the decision be adjusted if it fails?

A practical decision standard is:

> **Reasoning + Reality**

---

## Closure: Build Decisions That Can Be Revised

A Judging preference does not require permanent decisions.

Use:

> **Decision Version 1.0**

Record:

- current direction;
- evidence;
- assumptions;
- time horizon;
- success indicators;
- review date.

This allows forward movement without turning an early judgment into an irreversible commitment.
