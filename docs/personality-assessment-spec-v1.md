## 1. Executive summary

The InnerGeodessa Personality Preference Assessment is intended to be a structured personality-preference assessment designed for self-reflection, personal development, communication, learning, and career exploration. It is for people who want a practical vocabulary for considering how they currently direct energy, process information, make decisions, and approach structure.

The assessment is intended to support reflection and conversation. It will not diagnose a condition, measure intelligence or ability, make employment or admissions decisions, determine compatibility, or provide a definitive statement of identity. Results will describe self-reported preferences and should be considered alongside lived experience and other evidence.

A formal specification is required before a full question bank is written so that every future item can be traced to a defined construct, reviewed against consistent editorial and ethical standards, scored transparently, versioned correctly, and evaluated through pilot testing. This prevents the question bank, scoring engine, and report content from developing as disconnected systems.

The first version uses a four-dimension personality-preference framework inspired by established type-preference traditions while remaining an independent InnerGeodessa instrument. InnerGeodessa is not affiliated with or endorsed by The Myers-Briggs Company, the Myers & Briggs Foundation, or any other organisation.

Within the broader InnerGeodessa platform, the result may eventually become one clearly labelled perspective in a wider self-discovery profile alongside career interests, values, strengths, learning preferences, and symbolic zodiac content. No single assessment will be presented as a complete explanation of a person.

## 2. Intended use

### Appropriate uses

- Guided self-reflection and facilitated discussion.
- Reflection on communication preferences and misunderstandings.
- Exploration of learning preferences and study environments.
- Personal-development planning and journaling prompts.
- Career exploration when combined with interests, skills, qualifications, opportunities, and other evidence.
- Comparing how preferences may appear across contexts or change over time.
- Generating questions for discussion with a coach, educator, colleague, or trusted person.

### Prohibited or inappropriate uses

- Medical, mental-health, or psychological diagnosis.
- Treatment or care decisions.
- Hiring, firing, promotion, compensation, or recruitment screening.
- School, university, programme, or professional admissions decisions.
- Legal, medical, or financial decisions.
- Compatibility verdicts for partners, teams, friends, or families.
- Measuring intelligence, competence, aptitude, skill, or performance.
- Ranking one preference pattern as better, healthier, or more desirable than another.

Users should not make major personal, educational, career, relationship, medical, legal, or financial decisions solely from this assessment.

## 3. Theoretical model

The model contains four preference continua:

1. Extraversion ↔ Introversion (`EI`)
2. Sensing ↔ Intuition (`SN`)
3. Thinking ↔ Feeling (`TF`)
4. Judging ↔ Perceiving (`JP`)

Each dimension is a continuum rather than a binary possession of one trait and absence of another. Users receive a relative score on both sides. The leading side indicates the direction their responses currently favour within this assessment; it does not remove access to the opposite preference.

Behaviour may vary with context, role, culture, development, stress, safety, expectations, and experience. The four-letter summary is therefore an interpretive shorthand, not the complete report. The model measures self-reported preferences, not ability, achievement, moral character, or future performance.

### Naming and trademark note

`MBTI` is a trademarked term. Public-facing InnerGeodessa copy should use phrases such as:

- four-dimensional personality preference assessment;
- 16-type style profile; or
- personality preference pattern.

The InnerGeodessa assessment must not be described as an official MBTI assessment or as affiliated with its trademark owners.

## 4. Dimension definitions

### 4.1 Extraversion and Introversion

**Conceptual definition:** The preferred direction of attention and energy, including how a person tends to process experience, seek stimulation, interact, and recover.

| Aspect | Extraversion preference | Introversion preference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Energy orientation | Engagement with people, activity, and the external environment | Reflection, internal focus, and lower-stimulation space |
| Processing | Often thinks through speaking or interaction | Often reflects before speaking or sharing |
| Interaction | Often seeks breadth and varied contact | Often seeks depth and selective contact |
| Expression timing | May express developing thoughts earlier | May wait until thoughts feel formed |
| Stimulation | Often welcomes more external stimulation | Often prefers moderate or controlled stimulation |
| Recovery | May recharge through engagement | May recharge through solitude or quiet |

**Measures:** Relative preferences for energy renewal, external or internal processing, social breadth or depth, expression timing, stimulation, and initiation.

**Does not measure:** Social skill, confidence, shyness, popularity, loneliness, warmth, leadership, or communication ability.

**Common misinterpretations:** Extraversion is not “good with people,” and Introversion is not “antisocial.” People on either side may be skilled presenters, private, sociable, anxious, confident, or lonely.

**Proposed behavioural indicators:** Seeking conversation to clarify a thought; reserving time alone after an active day; initiating contact; preferring to observe before contributing.

**Contextual factors:** Familiarity, cultural conversational norms, role expectations, remote work, sensory environment, psychological safety, fatigue, and the closeness of relationships.

### 4.2 Sensing and Intuition

**Conceptual definition:** The preferred way of noticing, organising, and connecting information.

| Aspect | Sensing preference | Intuition preference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Information | Concrete observations and available facts | Patterns, implications, and possibilities |
| Time focus | Present realities and prior experience | Future potential and emerging direction |
| Attention | Specific detail and sequence | Relationships, themes, and the whole |
| Method | Established or demonstrable methods | Conceptual exploration and alternatives |
| Explanation | Practical examples and direct application | Theories, metaphors, and frameworks |
| Learning | Often sequential and grounded | Often associative and pattern-led |

**Measures:** Relative preferences for concrete or abstract information, detail or pattern, established method or possibility, and sequential or associative learning.

**Does not measure:** Intelligence, creativity, education, realism, imagination, memory, expertise, or capacity to use either information style.

**Common misinterpretations:** Sensing does not mean unimaginative; Intuition does not mean impractical. Neither side has exclusive access to facts, ideas, foresight, or creativity.

**Proposed behavioural indicators:** Asking for a worked example; noticing a discrepancy in details; proposing several future interpretations; connecting ideas across domains.

**Contextual factors:** Subject expertise, teaching style, task novelty, time pressure, occupational habits, language fluency, and whether accurate detail or broad exploration is rewarded.

### 4.3 Thinking and Feeling

**Conceptual definition:** The criteria a person tends to foreground when evaluating choices, fairness, feedback, conflict, and consequences.

| Aspect | Thinking preference | Feeling preference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Decision criteria | Impersonal consistency, principles, and logical consequences | Values, individual needs, and relational consequences |
| Fairness | Consistent standards and comparable treatment | Context-sensitive treatment and humane impact |
| Feedback | Direct critique focused on the issue | Feedback shaped around reception and relationship |
| Conflict | Tests positions and underlying logic | Considers values, needs, and relational effects |
| Evaluation | Looks for defensible reasoning | Looks for value alignment and human consequences |

**Measures:** Relative weighting of impersonal consistency and personal or relational impact in decisions.

**Does not measure:** Logical versus emotional, intelligent versus irrational, objective versus biased, kind versus unkind, or having versus lacking feelings.

**Common misinterpretations:** Thinking preferences do not imply emotional absence. Feeling preferences do not imply weak reasoning. Both preferences can be principled, compassionate, and analytical.

**Proposed behavioural indicators:** Applying a consistent decision rule; testing an argument through critique; adjusting a decision for individual circumstances; considering how feedback will affect trust.

**Contextual factors:** Stakes, closeness of relationships, organisational culture, power differences, professional codes, previous conflict, urgency, and perceived fairness.

### 4.4 Judging and Perceiving

**Conceptual definition:** The preferred relationship with planning, closure, adaptation, decision timing, and environmental structure.

| Aspect | Judging preference | Perceiving preference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Closure | Often values decisions and settled direction | Often values keeping options available |
| Planning | Often prefers plans and milestones | Often prefers adaptable frameworks |
| Structure | Often creates order before acting | Often discovers structure while acting |
| Timing | May decide earlier to enable execution | May wait for additional information |
| Tasks | Often uses schedules and completion sequences | Often works in responsive bursts or shifting priorities |
| Change | May prefer controlled revisions to a plan | May readily reconfigure plans as conditions change |

**Measures:** Relative preferences for closure or openness, advance structure or adaptation, and earlier or later decision timing.

**Does not measure:** Being judgmental versus perceptive, disciplined versus lazy, organised versus incompetent, or responsible versus irresponsible.

**Common misinterpretations:** Judging does not mean inflexible, and Perceiving does not mean unreliable. Either preference can plan, adapt, meet commitments, or struggle with organisation.

**Proposed behavioural indicators:** Creating milestones before starting; wanting decisions resolved; revising a plan when new options appear; delaying commitment to gather information.

**Contextual factors:** Workload, deadline design, consequences of error, autonomy, caregiving demands, executive-function supports, team norms, and unpredictability.

## 6. Assessment length and blueprint

### Recommended first full version

- 72 scored items.
- 18 items per main dimension.
- Approximately three items per subdimension.
- Estimated completion time: 8–12 minutes, subject to usability testing.
- One consistent response scale for all scored items.
- Items interleaved across dimensions and not displayed in obvious construct blocks.

| Dimension | Subdimensions | Proposed items | Approximate keyed balance | Proposed counter-keyed items |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- | ---: |
| `EI` | 6 | 18 | 9 E / 9 I | 6 |
| `SN` | 6 | 18 | 9 S / 9 N | 6 |
| `TF` | 6 | 18 | 9 T / 9 F | 6 |
| `JP` | 6 | 18 | 9 J / 9 P | 6 |
| **Total** | **24** | **72** | **36 left / 36 right** | **24** |

These counts are blueprint targets, not evidence that the resulting scales are balanced or valid.

### Optional future short form

A future short form may contain approximately 40–48 items. It is not part of V1.0 and may only be designed after sufficient pilot data. It must not be produced by simply deleting items; selection must consider construct coverage, item behaviour, reliability, burden, and comparability.

### Wording and scoring distinctions

- **Reverse direction:** An item is keyed toward the opposite pole from another item on the same dimension.
- **Reverse scoring:** The numeric response mapping is inverted before applying the scoring key.
- **Negatively worded item:** The sentence contains linguistic negation, such as “I do not…”. This is a wording property, not automatically a scoring property.

Counter-keying can reduce simple agreement patterns without requiring negative grammar. Exactly half of the items need not be negatively worded. Excessive negative wording should be avoided because it can increase comprehension errors.

## 7. Response scale

All scored items use:

1. Strongly disagree
2. Disagree
3. Neither agree nor disagree / Unsure
4. Agree
5. Strongly agree

The neutral option is retained because a person may feel genuinely balanced, lack sufficient context, or find neither direction representative. A neutral response is an intentional scored response of zero; an unanswered item is missing data and must not be treated as neutral.

Users may change answers before submission. The interface must not visually privilege a response, and labels must remain visible or programmatically accessible on mobile. Keyboard navigation, screen-reader labelling, visible focus, and practical touch targets are required.

### Provisional missing-answer rule

- All scored questions should normally be answered before completion.
- The interface should identify unanswered questions without deleting completed answers.
- V1.0 will not silently impute missing responses.
- If incomplete submission is later permitted, its scoring and reporting rules require a separately approved revision.

## 8. Item-writing standards

Every future item must:

- Measure one primary idea.
- Use plain international English.
- Describe a realistic tendency, preference, or behaviour.
- Avoid specialist occupational, academic, or psychological terminology.
- Be understandable without particular expertise.
- Avoid presenting one response as socially superior.
- Avoid deterministic wording and identity claims.
- Avoid requiring highly sensitive disclosure.
- Remain concise and culturally reviewable.
- Avoid assumptions about gender, family structure, religion, income, nationality, disability, technology access, or profession.

Items must avoid double-barrelled statements, double negatives, moralised language, obvious type labels, superficial duplicates, and unnecessary absolutes such as “always” or “never.” They must not primarily measure confidence, ability, mental health, social status, or access to a particular environment. Examples should not systematically advantage an occupational or cultural group and should not depend on references likely to date quickly.

**Illustrative strong item — not production copy:** “I often clarify an idea by discussing it with someone.”

This item describes one accessible processing tendency. It does not claim skill or virtue, although it still requires review for context and interpretation.

**Illustrative weak item — do not use:** “I am always a confident and inspiring speaker at large professional events.”

The weak item is double-barrelled, absolute, socially desirable, occupation-specific, dependent on access to large events, and confounds Extraversion with confidence and speaking ability.

This specification does not contain the production 72-item question bank.

```ts
    | "editorial-review"
    | "pilot"

    | "retired";
  reviewNotes?: string;

```

- **Keyed preference:** The pole toward which endorsement contributes after the scoring direction is applied.
- **Scoring direction:** Whether the centred response value is used as entered (`ascending`) or inverted (`descending`).
- **Wording type:** Whether the item follows the primary directional phrasing in its subdimension set or deliberately counters it. It does not indicate grammatical negativity.
- **Item status:** The controlled lifecycle stage; only `active` items belong in a public production bank.

Stable IDs use `P-{DIMENSION}-{SUBDIMENSION}-{SEQUENCE}`, for example `P-EI-ENERGY-001`. IDs do not change when wording changes. A wording revision increments the item `version`; material scoring or construct changes may also require a new item ID after review.

## 10. Question review process

1. Construct definition.
2. Item drafting.
3. Editorial review.
4. Bias and accessibility review.
5. Duplicate-content review.
6. Scoring-key review.
7. Small cognitive interview.
8. Pilot release.
9. Item analysis.
10. Revision, activation, or retirement.

Every item review must answer:

- What exactly does this item measure?
- Could it reasonably be interpreted in more than one way?
- Is one response socially desirable?
- Does it require a particular culture, lifestyle, resource, or role?
- Does it unintentionally measure ability?
- Does it duplicate another item?
- Is the keyed preference and scoring direction correct?
- Is the reading level appropriate?
- Could translation alter the intended construct?

AI-generated items require human editorial, construct, bias, accessibility, and psychometric review before production use.

## 11. Scoring model

This model is transparent and provisional.

### 11.1 Centred response

| Response | Base value |
| --- | ---: |
| Strongly disagree | `-2` |
| Disagree | `-1` |
| Neutral / Unsure | `0` |
| Agree | `+1` |
| Strongly agree | `+2` |

For item \(i\), let \(b_i\) be the base value. Apply scoring direction:

\[
d_i =
\begin{cases}
b_i & \text{if ascending}\\
-b_i & \text{if descending}
\end{cases}
\]

For each dimension, define the right pole as `I`, `N`, `F`, or `P`. Convert the item to a common right-positive axis:

\[
c_i =
\begin{cases}
d_i & \text{if keyed to the right pole}\\
-d_i & \text{if keyed to the left pole}
\end{cases}
\]

Thus \(c_i\) ranges from `-2` to `+2`, negative values support the left pole, and positive values support the right pole.

### 11.2 Dimension calculations

For \(n\) answered items in a complete V1.0 dimension, \(n = 18\):

\[
\text{netDirectionalScore} = N = \sum_{i=1}^{n} c_i
\]

\[
\text{leftRaw} = \sum_{i=1}^{n}(2-c_i)
\qquad
\text{rightRaw} = \sum_{i=1}^{n}(2+c_i)
\]

Each raw evidence score ranges from `0` to `4n`; together they sum to `4n`.

\[
\text{leftPercentage} = 100 \times \frac{\text{leftRaw}}{4n}
\qquad
\text{rightPercentage} = 100 \times \frac{\text{rightRaw}}{4n}
\]

- Leading preference: the pole with the higher unrounded percentage.
- Preference margin: absolute difference between the two unrounded percentages.
- Provisional preference strength: band assigned from the leading percentage.
- Exact tie: both unrounded percentages equal 50 and the dimension is recorded as balanced.

Percentages must be rounded using a deterministic largest-remainder correction so the displayed pair sums to exactly 100. For two values, round one consistently from the unrounded score and calculate the other as `100 - displayedFirst`. Calculations and tie detection use unrounded values.

These percentages are relative response directions within this assessment. They are not literal quantities of personality, ability, or behaviour.

### 11.3 Implementation-oriented pseudocode

```text
for each dimension:
  contributions = []

  for each answered item in dimension:
    base = mapResponseToCenteredValue(response)
    directed = scoringDirection == "ascending" ? base : -base
    contribution = keyedPreference == rightPole ? directed : -directed
    contributions.push(contribution)

  n = contributions.length
  net = sum(contributions)
  leftRaw = sum(2 - contribution)
  rightRaw = sum(2 + contribution)
  leftExact = 100 * leftRaw / (4 * n)
  rightExact = 100 * rightRaw / (4 * n)
  leftDisplay = round(leftExact)
  rightDisplay = 100 - leftDisplay
  leadingPreference = compare(leftExact, rightExact)
  margin = abs(leftExact - rightExact)
```

## 12. Preference-strength bands

| Leading percentage | Provisional label |
| --- | --- |
| 50–54 | Balanced / very slight |
| 55–59 | Slight |
| 60–69 | Moderate |
| 70–79 | Clear |
| 80–100 | Very clear |

The bands are provisional and must be reviewed against pilot score distributions and user comprehension. A higher percentage is not better. A close margin is not invalid, and a balanced response pattern may be meaningful. Frontend copy must avoid overstating precision.

Results should display both the leading preference and the full two-sided distribution, for example:

> Introversion 62% · Extraversion 38%

## 13. Confidence and response-quality indicators

Confidence is a cautious description of the response set, not a clinical validity scale and not a judgement about the user.

Potential indicators include completion rate, excessive neutral responding, unusually fast completion, straight-line responding, inconsistency among closely related items, contradictory counter-keyed responses, close dimension margins, long inactivity gaps if timing is retained, and repeated answer changes if available.

### Provisional decision table

| Observed pattern | Provisional output | User-facing interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Complete responses; no material quality flags; dimensions sufficiently interpretable | Higher confidence | Responses formed a comparatively consistent pattern in this session |
| One or two mild flags, or one or more close dimensions | Moderate confidence | Some preferences are clear while others may benefit from reflection |
| Multiple material flags, extensive neutral use, very rapid completion, or widespread close margins | Exploratory result | Treat this result as an initial perspective and consider retaking thoughtfully |

Exact thresholds for “excessive,” “unusually fast,” “inconsistent,” and “material” cannot be finalised before usability and pilot testing. Response-quality flags must not automatically invalidate a result. Contradictions can reflect context, ambivalence, item wording, or genuine flexibility rather than carelessness.

Use `Higher confidence`, `Moderate confidence`, and `Exploratory result`. Do not use `valid personality`, `invalid person`, `deceptive respondent`, or `dishonest result`. Explanations must be respectful and disclose the relevant basis without implying hidden clinical validity.

## 14. Result-code generation

For each dimension:

1. Compare unrounded pole percentages.
2. Select the leading preference when one side leads.
3. When exactly tied, record an internal balanced marker: `X` for that dimension and add the dimension to `balancedDimensions`.
4. Do not silently force a side or remove the tie from canonical data.
5. Public-facing V1.0 may show the closest matching four-letter pattern only if it also identifies balanced dimensions and explains the tie rule.

If public display requires a letter for an exact tie, the provisional deterministic rule is to choose the pole supported by the greater count of non-neutral item responses; if that also ties, use the first pole in the documented dimension order solely for the closest-match code. The canonical result retains the balanced marker regardless. This presentation rule remains open for pilot review.

Example:

> Closest matching profile: INTJ-style

Preferred copy:

- “Your current preference pattern most closely resembles INTJ.”
- “This dimension was close to balanced.”
- “Your responses currently lean toward Introversion.”

Avoid:

- “You are definitely INTJ.”
- “This is your permanent type.”
- “You are 72% intuitive as a person.”

## 20. Data and privacy principles

- Collect only data necessary to deliver and improve the assessment.
- Explain clearly what is processed, stored, retained, and shared.
- Distinguish anonymous or local results from account-linked results.
- Do not require an account for the initial assessment unless product requirements change transparently.
- Do not retain raw answers indefinitely without a documented purpose and retention period.
- Allow users to download their report.
- Use opaque result IDs that contain no personal information.
- Never place sensitive answers or result payloads in URLs.
- Do not claim legal or regulatory compliance before implementation and review.
- Prepare deletion, consent, access, and correction controls if accounts are introduced.

Detailed storage, security, retention, consent, and legal architecture belongs in a separate future privacy specification.

## 21. Versioning

| Artefact | Suggested version | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Assessment model | `personality-v1` | Defines constructs and intended interpretation |
| Question bank | `pqb-1.0.0` | Identifies the exact active item set |
| Individual item | Integer version per stable ID | Tracks wording and key revisions |
| Scoring model | `psm-1.0.0` | Identifies formulas, thresholds, and tie rules |
| Content library | `pcl-1.0.0` | Identifies reviewed report modules |
| Report template | `prt-1.0.0` | Identifies web/PDF presentation structure |

Versions remain separate because changing wording is not the same as changing a construct, scoring rule, interpretation module, or report layout. Separate identifiers make historical results reproducible and permit targeted review.

Semantic versioning:

- **Patch:** Wording or formatting correction with no intended scoring or construct impact.
- **Minor:** Compatible content, item, or presentation revision that preserves the assessment model.
- **Major:** Construct, scoring, comparability, or interpretation change.

Historical results and reports must preserve every version used at generation time.

## 22. Pilot-testing plan

### Stage A: Internal editorial review

- Review construct and subdimension coverage.
- Independently verify keys and scoring directions.
- Remove duplicate, leading, moralised, or inaccessible items.
- Test the complete flow and answer-review behaviour.

### Stage B: Cognitive interviews

- Use a small, varied group.
- Ask participants to explain what they think each item means.
- Identify ambiguous, confusing, sensitive, or culturally narrow wording.
- Record interpretation differences rather than treating them as participant errors.

### Stage C: Initial pilot

- Recruit a sufficiently varied sample, with requirements set in a future pilot protocol.
- Review completion time, missing responses, answer changes, and usability feedback.
- Examine dimension and item response distributions.
- Gather qualitative feedback on report clarity and overclaiming.

### Stage D: Statistical review

Future analysis may include:

- Item-total correlation.
- Internal consistency.
- Inter-item correlation.
- Response distributions.
- Floor and ceiling effects.
- Subdimension coverage.
- Exploratory factor analysis when sample size is sufficient.
- Test-retest reliability when repeat data is available.
- Differential item functioning and bias review when data allows.

Provisional review thresholds may be documented in the pilot protocol as decision aids for investigation. They will not, by themselves, prove reliability, validity, fairness, or fitness for use. No achieved reliability or validation target is claimed here.

### Stage E: Revision

- Revise or retire weak, confusing, redundant, or biased items.
- Rebalance subdimensions where evidence supports change.
- Review scoring thresholds and confidence rules.
- Revise report wording and explanations.
- Record every change with version and rationale.

## 24. Accessibility and inclusion

Requirements include:

- Plain-language instructions and concise items.
- Full keyboard operation and logical focus order.
- Screen-reader labels, groups, instructions, and error descriptions.
- Visible focus states and adequate colour contrast.
- No meaning communicated only through colour, position, or animation.
- Readable mobile controls with accessible labels and practical touch targets.
- No timed completion pressure.
- Ability to review and change answers before submission.
- Reduced-motion support.
- Culturally neutral examples where possible.
- Respectful, non-hierarchical language for every preference.

Translation must include construct and cultural review rather than word-for-word replacement. Each translated item set requires separate editorial review, cognitive interviewing, and pilot testing. Language versions must be versioned independently while retaining traceability to source items.

## 25. Ethical content rules

These rules are non-negotiable:

- No type is superior.
- No type predicts moral character.
- No type determines intelligence.
- No type guarantees career success.
- No type determines relationship compatibility.
- No type is associated with mental illness.
- No result should shame, frighten, or alarm a user.
- Balanced dimensions are valid.
- Users may disagree with their result.
- Users should compare the result with lived experience.
- Language must leave room for development, agency, role, and context.

## 26. Future integration with InnerGeodessa

The Personality result may eventually connect with:

- RIASEC career interests.
- Values and strengths.
- Learning and communication preferences.
- Personal goals and reflective journal entries.
- Zodiac identity as explicitly symbolic content.

Integration boundaries:

- Do not use simple deterministic matching.
- Do not claim that a combined profile fully explains a person.
- Surface contradictions between assessments as useful questions, not errors.
- Career suggestions must distinguish interests, preferences, skills, qualifications, constraints, and labour-market realities.
- Keep symbolic zodiac content visibly separate from evidence-oriented assessment content.
